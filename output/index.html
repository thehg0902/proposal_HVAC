Profile the buyer

Who your customer is

Before any ad gets written, it pays to know what your customer is living with and what they are hoping for.

Where they are now

  • Their furnace failed in the dead of winter.
  • A problem furnace has been bugging them for a while.
  • A filter stuck with calcium build-up, inside the unit and out.
  • Dust to clear every day, from construction material a past cleaning should have removed.

Where they want to be

  • No more worry at the back of their head.
  • Their family gets through winter and summer in peace.
  • Someone does the job clean, and no more worries.
  • No HVAC worries for years, just a routine annual check-up.

Profile the buyer

What your customers are saying

What earns praise

Market research

What's stopping them

Most people who need you don't call yet. Here is what holds them back, what they try first, and how they finally choose.

Not broken yet

Nothing is broken yet, so the fix doesn't feel worth the cost.

Not painful enough

The pain isn't bad enough to pull the trigger.

Waiting for an offer

It's a big spend, so they wait for the right offer.

The options they weigh

  1. Do it themselves. Low cost, but possible safety concerns and future problems.
  2. Ask "the guy." Low cost, but a high chance it doesn't get fixed.
  3. Do nothing. Wait another year until it gets truly unacceptable.
  4. Call a contractor. Get a free assessment, then see if they can fix it themselves.
  5. Pay a professional. They decide they can't fix it, and the relief is worth the money.

Already tried: changing their furnace settings · searching YouTube

What they look for

  • Good reviews.
  • Trustworthy, and gets the job done.
  • Fast response time.
  • Listens to them and doesn't rip them off.
  • No future issues: money spent that lasts for years, with annual maintenance.

What makes them distrust you

  • Slow response time.
  • Unprofessional people: how you dress, how you show up, how you follow up.
  • Bad reviews.

The opportunity

The gap

Almost nobody is using the customer's pain point.

What your customer feels

  • Their furnace failing in winter.
  • Worry at the back of their head.
  • Wanting the job done right.

What competitors lead with

  • Deals and deadlines.
  • Rebates.
  • Years in business.
  • Licensed & insured.

The feelings nobody names

  • The feeling of not getting it done properly.
  • The feeling of desperately trying to find someone in winter.

The approach

A stress-free, consultant approach that talks about the feelings that hit them.

Hooks that use the pain

  • Home feeling cold already?
  • Is your heating always inconsistent?
  • Are you prepared for the winter?

Engineering

How your customer decides

The path from a relaxed scroll to a phone call, step by step, with the doubt that has to be answered on the way.

  1. Scrolling after a day of work.
  2. A short curiosity hook.
  3. That's interesting, I probably have the same issue. (unsure)
  4. The pain, amplified: You don't want your kids to be freezing in winter and desperately trying to find someone, do you?
  5. Objection So what do you guys offer differently that we can trust you?
  6. A stress-free interview approach.
  7. That might sound like something we can maybe try out. Desire 7 Certainty 4 Trust 7
  8. Testimonials, previous customer feedback and past work.
  9. Call to action Make sure you and your family stay warm this winter.
