# Niche Playbooks — visual direction defaults per niche

One block per niche. Fields are fixed: **Converts on / Direction / Hero /
Must-have sections / Avoid**. Read ONE block (the one the niche-resolution
line named), never the file. Rows 6-16 are distilled from the matching
audience study's section 9 — when both exist, they agree by construction.

A playbook is a STARTING POSITION, not the direction. design-direction rule
5c still applies: if a competitor could paste the result unchanged, it is not
a direction yet.

---

## coffee-shop
- Converts on: atmosphere + location + hours. Visitors decide in seconds.
- Direction: warm neutrals or rich earth tones; one serif or humanist
  display face; generous whitespace; photography of space/drinks/people
  over product grids.
- Hero: ambient video or a warm still of the room.
- Must-have sections: hero, menu, gallery, contact (hours + map +
  Instagram), cta. Hours prominent in header AND contact.
- Avoid: stock latte-art cliches; dark moody palettes for family cafes.
  Conversion is directions + menu, not forms.

## restaurant
- Converts on: menu + reservations + food photography quality.
- Direction: palette from the cuisine's own culture (ask before
  stereotyping); high-contrast food imagery; elegant serif upscale, bold
  sans casual.
- Hero: signature dish or dining-room video.
- Must-have sections: hero, menu (HTML, not PDF-only — SEO), gallery,
  about, contact (hours + map), cta (reserve/call above the fold on mobile).
- Avoid: music, autoplaying sound, PDF-only menus.

## gym
- Converts on: vibe match + trial offer + schedule clarity.
- Direction: high energy = bold condensed type, dark bg, one electric
  accent; boutique/yoga = light, airy, calm. Real members over equipment.
- Hero: training footage — motion sells energy.
- Must-have sections: hero, services (classes/schedule), about (coaches'
  faces), testimonials, cta (trial offer), contact. Ask the client whether
  pricing is shown or inquired.
- Avoid: stock bodybuilders; aggressive tone for wellness studios.

## hvac
- Converts on: trust + speed of contact. The visitor is often mid-breakdown.
- Direction: clean blues/neutrals + one warm accent; sturdy sans;
  straightforward layout, zero cleverness.
- Hero: static or subtle — big headline stating service + area + phone.
- Must-have sections: hero (phone visible, tap-to-call), services, about
  (licences + service area), testimonials, contact (short quote form:
  name/phone/issue), cta. 24-7 badge only if genuinely true.
- Avoid: long forms, animation-heavy pages, ambiguous CTAs.

## dental
- Converts on: calm trust + easy booking. Visitors are often anxious.
- Direction: soft light palette (avoid the clinical white-blue cliche —
  warm neutrals + teal/sage work), rounded radii, real team photos over
  stock smiles.
- Hero: static or very subtle motion; reassuring headline.
- Must-have sections: hero, services (plain language, not jargon), about
  (team + credentials), testimonials, faq (insurance/payment), contact
  (book CTA), cta.
- Avoid: graphic procedure imagery, fear-based copy.

## roofing
- Converts on: fear relief + proof of legitimacy. Fear-dominant niche —
  nobody wants a roof, they want the problem gone.
- Direction: grounded neutrals, one strong trust accent; sturdy sans;
  photography of real crews and finished roofs, shot locally.
- Hero: DUAL-PATH split — "I have a leak now" vs "I'm planning a
  replacement". Fear-relief copy beats aspiration.
- Must-have sections: hero (dual path), trust stack near the fold (years +
  town, review count, WSIB/liability, manufacturer certification, written
  warranty), services, before/after gallery by neighbourhood, honest
  repair-vs-replace guide, cost-transparency page, contact (free
  inspection CTA + emergency line, sticky call on mobile).
- Avoid: storm-chaser aesthetics, deposit vagueness, generic stock roofs.

## electrician
- Converts on: licence proof + price transparency + response speed.
- Direction: clean, orderly, high-contrast; sturdy sans; photos of tidy
  work (labelled panels, shoe covers) as competence proof.
- Hero: DUAL-LANE — "Emergency? Call now" (sticky tap-to-call) vs "Book a
  project".
- Must-have sections: hero (dual lane), ECRA/ESA licence number sitewide
  in header/footer, price-transparency block (service-call fee + menu
  ranges + firm-quote promise), services, EV-charger page, insurance
  remediation pages (aluminum, knob-and-tube), trust stack, contact.
- Avoid: hiding the service-call fee; upsell-flavoured copy — the winning
  position is "we'll tell you when you DON'T need it".

## garage-door-repair
- Converts on: same-day availability + published prices. Designed for the
  7 a.m. panic scroll.
- Direction: mobile-first and blunt; large type; high-contrast; local
  photography (wrapped vans on recognizable streets), owner/tech faces.
- Hero: giant sticky click-to-call, "SAME-DAY SERVICE", hours (including
  evenings/weekends) visible instantly.
- Must-have sections: hero, published price ranges ("the price we quote is
  the price you pay"), symptom-based service pages (won't open / loud bang
  / won't close / frozen shut), local-proof stack, spring-safety block,
  warranty terms, separate calmer replacement/design lane, contact.
- Avoid: any extra tap between panic and phone; bait pricing; burying the
  high-ticket replacement buyer under the emergency UX.

## paving
- Converts on: dramatic before/after + written, itemized quoting.
- Direction: outdoor daylight photography, wide full-bleed imagery;
  confident plain sans; earthy neutrals with one authority accent.
- Hero: full-width before/after slider of a LOCAL driveway.
- Must-have sections: hero (before/after), gallery tagged by town,
  spec-transparency cross-section (base depth, lifts, compaction),
  anti-scam education page, trust stack (years, local address, branded
  trucks, WSIB, warranty term), seasonal urgency banner, sealcoating page,
  contact (free WRITTEN quote CTA).
- Avoid: unlabelled stock asphalt, vague warranty language, cash-only cues.

## concrete-contractor
- Converts on: craftsmanship detail + longevity proof.
- Direction: texture-forward — close-ups of edges, joints, finishes;
  restrained industrial palette; grounded sans; full-width photography.
- Hero: finish-led still ("poured once, perfect for 30 years").
- Must-have sections: hero, finish-first gallery (broom / exposed
  aggregate / stamped, location-tagged), crack/spall education page,
  spec-transparency cross-section, warranty clarity, trust stack (years
  pouring locally, WSIB, crew mid-pour), repair lane, concrete-vs-asphalt
  comparison, contact (itemized quote CTA, 48-hr promise).
- Avoid: glossy renders instead of real pours; vague warranty terms.

## tree-service
- Converts on: insurance proof + visible competence.
- Direction: outdoor, high-contrast, safety-forward; strong sans; video
  and action photography (rigging, crane picks, climbers).
- Hero: DUAL-LANE — storm emergency (sticky call) vs free assessment —
  with the insurance bar hero-adjacent ("$5M liability + WSIB,
  certificates before we start").
- Must-have sections: hero (dual lane + insurance bar), competence video
  or rigging gallery, cleanup before/during/after triptych, Emerald Ash
  Borer page, permit-navigation block per service area, honest-triage
  positioning, trust stack (ISA Certified Arborist, crew faces,
  service-area map), pruning-program page, contact.
- Avoid: hiding insurance details; remove-everything framing; stock
  chainsaw imagery.

## kitchen-bath-reno
- Converts on: gallery quality + budget certainty. The gallery IS the site.
- Direction: editorial and photography-led; generous whitespace; warm
  material palette drawn from the work itself; refined serif/sans pairing.
- Hero: a before/after slider or a single hero-grade finished room.
- Must-have sections: hero, filterable before/after gallery (room, style,
  neighbourhood — sliders beat static), numbered process page WITH
  durations, "how we protect your budget" block (fixed quotes, allowance
  transparency, change-order policy), 3D-render offer, dust/disruption
  proof, trust stack (video testimonials in finished rooms, WSIB,
  warranty, permits), contact (free design consultation CTA).
- Avoid: thin or phone-shot galleries — weak photos kill this niche;
  "get a quote" as the primary CTA.

## home-builder
- Converts on: reputation. This is a reputation site, not a lead-gen site —
  a cheap-looking site reads as a builder who cuts corners.
- Direction: the most premium build in the library — restrained, spacious,
  old-money minimal; high-contrast serif display; architectural
  photography with room to breathe.
- Hero: a single architectural image + a legacy-register headline.
- Must-have sections: hero, portfolio as mini case studies (sq ft,
  neighbourhood, timeline, 10-20 photos, client quote, filterable),
  transparency pages (what it costs to build here, fixed-price vs
  cost-plus, our process with phase durations, draws and deposits),
  communication-proof block, video testimonials, trust stack (HCRA licence
  + Tarion registration in footer and about, years, homes completed,
  owner's story), multigenerational/in-law-suite page where relevant,
  contact (Book a Discovery Call — never "Get a Quote").
- Avoid: commodity framing, stock interiors, anything that reads as a
  template.

## med-spa
- Converts on: taste as a proxy for safety. The site's restraint IS the
  argument that you will not get botched.
- Direction: the strongest fit in the library for calm editorial
  minimalism — generous whitespace, serif elegance, muted palette, quiet
  motion. A discount-styled site is disqualifying at this price point.
- Hero: a calm portrait or textural still + an anti-"done" line
  ("You, rested." / "Refreshed. Never frozen.").
- Must-have sections: hero, concern-led navigation (I look tired / lines /
  pigment & tone / unwanted hair / skin quality) rather than procedure
  acronyms, injector-as-product bios (portraits, training lineage,
  philosophy, named medical director), before/after gallery organized by
  concern AND age/skin tone, transparent price ranges, safety and
  medical-oversight block, discretion signals, frictionless online
  booking, contact.
- Avoid: dramatic transformations (they hurt here), filtered or
  inconsistent-lighting before/afters, phone-only booking, discount
  styling.

## physiotherapy
- Converts on: online booking with live availability + direct billing.
- Direction: clean, bright, human; approachable sans; photography of
  hands-on treatment and real practitioners, not equipment.
- Hero: booking-first with hours as a headline ("Open evenings &
  Saturdays"), not a footnote.
- Must-have sections: hero (book CTA in header, hero, and every condition
  page), insurer-logo wall + "direct billing — you pay nothing upfront",
  "no referral needed" myth-buster line, condition pages written
  symptom-first (low back, neck, sciatica, frozen shoulder, plantar
  fasciitis, knee, rotator cuff, concussion, vertigo, post-surgical,
  pelvic health), practitioner bios with credentials and book-by-
  practitioner, the endpoint promise ("most patients need 6-10 visits"),
  MVA/WSIB page, contact.
- Avoid: call-us-only funnels; photocopied-exercise-sheet energy; burying
  hours.

## chiropractor
- Converts on: same-day booking + visible honesty about the treatment plan.
- Direction: professional and restrained — the tone IS the legitimacy
  argument; evidence-toned, zero woo; calm neutral palette, clinical-warm.
- Hero: same-day availability stated as a feature, one-thumb bookable.
- Must-have sections: hero (same-day + weekend hours), the anti-money-trap
  block ("pay per visit, no prepaid plans, no contracts — most acute
  episodes resolve in 3-5 visits and we'll tell you when you're done"),
  transparent pricing + insurer logos + direct billing, legitimacy stack
  (CCO registration, credentials, years, co-located physio/massage if
  true), gentle-technique page (Activator, drop table, mobilization),
  symptom-first condition pages, quiet separate wellness/maintenance lane,
  contact.
- Avoid: subluxation cosmology, program-selling language anywhere near the
  acute funnel, hidden pricing.
